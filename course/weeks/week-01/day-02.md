# Day 2 — Digital Thinking: Observe Real-World Problems

## Learning Objective

By the end of today, students can **observe a real-world situation**, name the **people**, **pain**, and **current process**, and write a clear **problem statement** (not a technology wishlist). They understand that good apps start from **problems people already have**.

No coding. Fix-My-Ride remains reference only.

---

## Materials

- Day 1 Layer Maps (reuse if available)
- “Problem Observation Card” worksheet (print 2 per student)
- Sticky notes
- Timer visible to class
- Trainer copy of Fix-My-Ride problem paragraph ([FIX-MY-RIDE.md](../../FIX-MY-RIDE.md) — Problem section only)
- Optional: photos of paper registers / WhatsApp groups (anonymised) as discussion prompts

---

## PART 1 — THEORY (~1 hour)

### Concept

**Digital thinking** means looking at ordinary life and asking:

1. Who is struggling?  
2. What are they trying to do?  
3. How do they do it today (paper, memory, calls, Excel, WhatsApp)?  
4. Where does information get lost, delayed, or argued?  
5. Could a simple digital system make the *process* clearer — without boiling the ocean?

It is **not**: “What cool AI feature can I add?”  
It is **yes**: “Where is the pain in the process?”

---

### Simple Explanation

Before a doctor gives medicine, they **observe symptoms**.  
Before we build an app, we **observe the problem**.

Many beginners start with:

> “I want to build an app with login, maps, chat, payments, and AI.”

That is a **feature shopping list**, not a problem.

A problem sounds like:

> “When a customer leaves a bike for service, details discussed at the counter are forgotten if another person handles the bike later. There is no single clear service-process record.”

That second sentence is Fix-My-Ride’s **reference** problem — students will write *their own* in the same style.

---

### Analogy

| Weak start | Strong start |
|------------|--------------|
| “I want a smart hospital app” | “Patients wait without knowing token status; staff rewrite the same details on 3 papers” |
| “I want a student super-app” | “Assignment deadlines live in 4 WhatsApp groups; students miss dates” |
| “I want Flipkart” | “Our small shop loses track of who ordered what by phone” |

**Analogy:** Building without observation is like packing a suitcase without knowing the trip. You carry random heavy things and still forget the passport.

---

### Why our app needs this

C-VERSE rule from the course overview:

> The student's problem determines the application.  
> C-VERSE determines whether and how that application becomes a suitable learning project.

If the problem is unclear:

- Entities become random  
- Scope explodes  
- Week 8 has half of everything and all of nothing  

Observation today feeds Days 3–5 (scope, entities, Transfer Sheet).

---

### Small Example

**Scene:** A tuition centre tracks fees in a notebook + parent WhatsApp messages.

| Observation question | Sample answer |
|----------------------|---------------|
| Who? | Admin staff, parents, students |
| Goal? | Know who paid / who pending for this month |
| Today’s process? | Notebook ticks + “Sir, I paid yesterday” chats |
| Pain? | Arguments, missing ticks, no search, no history |
| Digital opportunity? | Simple fee-status record with search — not a full ERP |

Trainer emphasises: we observed **fee tracking pain**, not “let’s build Zoom + LMS + AI tutor”.

---

### Important Terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|------|----------------|
| Real-world problem | A difficulty people face in life/work today |
| Stakeholder / user | A person who uses or is affected by the process |
| Current process | How work happens *now* (even if messy) |
| Pain point | Where time, money, trust, or information is lost |
| Problem statement | 3–5 clear sentences describing the pain (not the tech stack) |
| Solution idea | Later step — do not jump here too fast |
| Feature wishlist | List of cool buttons without problem — avoid today |
| Reference problem | Fix-My-Ride’s bike-service memory problem (demo only) |

---

### Trainer Script

#### What to say (opening — 5 minutes)

> “Yesterday we learned: an application has input, rules, memory, and output.  
> Today we ask a harder question: *Which problem is worth digitising?*  
> Look around your life. Colleges, shops, clinics, hostels, family businesses, NGOs, sports clubs — everywhere people still lose information.  
> Your job today is not to invent Silicon Valley. Your job is to **observe**.”

Write on board:

```text
PROBLEM FIRST → APP LATER
```

#### What to show (10 minutes)

Read the Fix-My-Ride problem (short):

> Bike service is largely manual… details discussed can be forgotten… the shop needs a clear digital service-process record.

Then explicitly say:

> “This is Hari’s bike-shop story for *our classroom demo*. Do not all become bike-shop apps. Steal the *style* of the problem statement — not the bikes.”

Show a bad problem statement on the board:

> “I will build an app using React, FastAPI, AI chatbot, Google Maps, and payment gateway.”

Cross it out. Replace with a good skeleton:

```text
Who has the problem?
What are they trying to do?
What goes wrong today?
What information gets lost?
```

#### What to ask (15–20 minutes)

Cold-call gently around the room:

1. “Name one place you visited this week where someone wrote the same details twice.”  
2. “Where do people say ‘I told you already’ because nothing was recorded?”  
3. “Is ‘I want dark mode’ a problem statement? Why not?”  
4. “If two users fight about facts, what is usually missing — a nicer colour, or a shared record?”  
5. “Can a problem be real but still too big for eight weeks?” (Preview Day 3: yes.)

#### Expected answers

| Prompt | Direction |
|--------|-----------|
| Same details twice | Hospital forms, college office, hotel check-in, service centres |
| “I told you already” | Verbal-only processes, WhatsApp chaos, memory-based shops |
| Dark mode | Preference / polish — not core problem |
| Fight about facts | Missing shared trusted record (database need!) |
| Too big | Yes — Flipkart-scale; we cut on Day 3 |

#### Common confusion

| Confusion | Correction |
|-----------|------------|
| Jumping to UI wireframes immediately | “Hold the pencils for screens — finish the pain first.” |
| Copying Fix-My-Ride wording with ‘car’ instead of ‘bike’ | “That’s still their problem, not yours. Find *your* observation.” |
| Choosing a problem they have never seen | “Prefer problems you can explain from real life.” |
| “My problem is I don’t know coding” | “That’s a learning goal, not an app problem for users.” |
| Mixing 5 domains into one app | “Pick one world for Week 1.” |

#### Correction lines

> “If you cannot explain the problem to your grandmother in simple words, you do not understand it yet.”

> “Technology names are not problems. Lost information, delayed work, and unclear status *are* problems.”

> “Copying the demo idea teaches you less than observing your own world.”

> “One clear problem beats five vague dreams.”

#### Mid-theory micro-practice (10 minutes)

Students draft **one sentence** only:

> “In __________, people struggle because __________.”

Share 4 aloud. Trainer praises clarity, not ambition.

#### Closing theory line

> “This afternoon you will fill Observation Cards. Tomorrow we turn problems into **scoped solutions** — base now, enhance later.”

## PART 2 — PRACTICAL (~1 hour)

### Starting Point

Students understand problem-first thinking.  
They may have a rough idea from the one-sentence drill.  
Still no Transfer Sheet sign-off; still no code.

---

### Activity A — Silent observation list (10 minutes)

Individually, list **5 places/processes** from real life (college, home, work, neighbourhood).  
Examples of *places*, not solutions: canteen billing, lab equipment issue, salon appointments, PG rent collection, NGO donor list.

Trainer: “Quantity first. Quality later.”

---

### Activity B — Observation Card #1 (20 minutes)

Each student picks **one** item from their list and completes Card #1:

| Field | Guidance |
|-------|----------|
| Place / domain | Where does this happen? |
| People involved | Max 4 names/roles |
| What they want to achieve | Goal in one line |
| How it works today | 4–6 steps, honest and messy |
| What goes wrong | At least 2 pain points |
| What information is lost or delayed | Be specific |
| How often this pain happens | Daily / weekly / monthly |
| Draft problem statement | 3–5 sentences — **no tech stack words** |

**Banned words on Card #1:** React, FastAPI, AI, blockchain, MongoDB, “I will use…”

Trainer walk-around: strike tech words kindly; ask “What fails for the *human*?”

---

### Activity C — Partner challenge (15 minutes)

Pairs swap cards.

Listener asks only these questions (trainer posts them):

1. “Who suffers most?”  
2. “What would ‘better’ look like in one sentence — without naming an app feature?”  
3. “Is any part of this problem only a rumour you never actually saw?”  
4. “If we could fix only *one* pain this course, which pain?”

Writer revises problem statement after feedback (5 minutes).

---

### Activity D — Gallery + Fix-My-Ride contrast (10 minutes)

3 volunteers read problem statements.

Trainer places Fix-My-Ride problem beside them and asks:

> “Do you hear the same *shape*? People + process + forgotten/lost information + need for a clear record?”

If a statement is actually a feature list, class rewrites it live on the board.

---

### Activity E — Second card seed (5 minutes)

Students briefly note **Card #2 candidate** (backup idea) in case Day 3–5 kills scope on idea #1.  
No full write-up required unless they finish early.

---

## Student Checkpoint (5–10 questions)

1. What should come first — problem or technology choice?  
2. Name two signs of a weak “problem statement”.  
3. What is a pain point?  
4. Why do we ask “how it works today”?  
5. True or false: Every real problem is automatically a good 8-week project.  
6. Rewrite this into a problem tone: “I want login, chat, and payments.” (one better sentence)  
7. Who owns the idea in C-VERSE — student or trainer?  
8. What does the trainer own?  
9. Give one example of information getting lost in a manual process.  
10. Why keep a backup idea (Card #2 seed)?

**Suggested answers:** 1 Problem · 2 Feature wishlist / only tech names / no people · 3 Where process hurts · 4 To see real steps and gaps · 5 False (scope Day 3) · 6 e.g. People cannot track order status across phone calls · 7 Student · 8 Curriculum and scope fitness · 9 Verbal notes forgotten, paper misplaced · 10 If idea #1 cannot be baselined

---

## Must Understand / Must Build

### Must Understand

- Digital thinking = observe people and process  
- Problem statement ≠ feature wishlist  
- Current process matters  
- Fix-My-Ride problem is a *style model*, not a mandatory domain  
- Prefer problems you can explain from experience  

### Must Build

- List of 5 real-life processes  
- Observation Card #1 with 3–5 sentence problem statement (no tech stack)  
- Partner feedback revision  
- Backup idea seed  

---

## Do Not Teach Yet

- BASE vs Enhance Later deep cut (introduce lightly only if students ask; full treatment Day 3)  
- Entity / ER diagrams (Day 4)  
- Qualification rubric scoring (Day 5)  
- UI design, wireframing tools  
- Database schema design  
- Any coding  

---

## Trainer Watch-outs

- Students picking “Uber for X” clones they have never lived  
- Romanticising problems they cannot describe in steps  
- Shame around “small” local problems — **small local is perfect**  
- Trainer accidentally pushing everyone toward bike service  
- Over-helping by writing their problem for them — ask questions instead  
- Time sink on perfect English — clarity > grammar; allow Tamil draft then English transfer
